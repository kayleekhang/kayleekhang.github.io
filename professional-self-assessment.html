Professional Self-Assessment

Completing the Computer Science program and developing this ePortfolio has helped me better understand both my technical strengths and my professional goals. Throughout the program, I have gained experience in software design, algorithms, databases, embedded systems, and problem solving. Just as important, I have learned how to evaluate my own work, improve existing systems, and communicate technical ideas more clearly. Building this ePortfolio allowed me to reflect on that growth and present my work in a way that demonstrates both technical ability and professional readiness.

One of the most valuable parts of the program was learning how to move beyond simply creating programs that work and instead focus on creating systems that are maintainable, scalable, and useful in realistic settings. My coursework helped me strengthen my foundation in programming and system design, while the capstone experience pushed me to revisit earlier projects and improve them with a more mature understanding of computer science concepts. This process shaped my professional goals by reinforcing my interest in software development, especially in areas related to embedded systems and practical system design.

Collaborating in a Team Environment

Throughout the program, I learned that collaboration is not limited to group coding projects. It also includes designing work in a way that others can understand, maintain, and extend. As I improved my artifacts, I thought about how another developer, manager, or stakeholder would view the code and whether they would be able to follow the design decisions I made. This reinforced the importance of organization, documentation, and clarity in a shared development environment.

Communicating With Stakeholders

The program also strengthened my ability to communicate technical information to different audiences. In assignments, project writeups, and the code review process, I had to explain not only what the software did but also why specific enhancements mattered. This helped me practice translating technical details into language that would be meaningful to instructors, peers, managers, or clients. I learned that strong communication is a critical part of software development because successful systems must be understood by both technical and nontechnical stakeholders.

Data Structures and Algorithms

My coursework helped me develop a stronger understanding of how algorithms and data-driven logic influence software behavior. In my thermostat enhancement, I improved the control logic by introducing hysteresis, which created more stable and realistic thermostat behavior. More broadly, the program taught me to think critically about efficiency, system behavior, and how small logic changes can significantly improve performance and reliability. This strengthened my ability to design solutions that are not only functional but also more thoughtful and effective.

Software Engineering and Databases

One of my greatest areas of growth was learning how to design software in a more structured and maintainable way. My ePortfolio artifacts demonstrate this through the refactoring of a monolithic embedded system into separate modules with clearly defined responsibilities. This process reinforced software engineering concepts such as modularity, composition, and separation of concerns.

I also grew in my understanding of databases by integrating SQLite into the thermostat project. Rather than storing all information in a simple flat structure, I separated settings and logs into multiple related tables. This helped me better understand relational design, CRUD operations, and the role of data persistence in real applications. Together, these experiences helped me see how software engineering and databases support each other in building useful, maintainable systems.

Security

Security is another important concept that has shaped my understanding of software development throughout the program. Secure design requires thinking beyond functionality and considering reliability, constraints, validation, and system integrity. In database work, this included thinking carefully about valid inputs and structured storage. In embedded and systems-focused work, it involved understanding that software interacts closely with hardware, communication channels, and operating environments. These experiences helped me appreciate that secure and dependable software must be intentionally designed rather than treated as an afterthought.

Growth Throughout the Program

Across the program, I developed a stronger ability to analyze code, improve designs, and connect computer science concepts to practical systems. In addition to the artifacts included in this ePortfolio, my coursework exposed me to a broad range of experiences that strengthened my skills. These experiences included working with embedded systems, applying software design principles, using database concepts in practical projects, and learning how to reflect critically on the quality and structure of my own work. Together, these experiences helped prepare me to become more employable and more confident in entering the computer science field.

How the Artifacts Fit Together

The artifacts in this portfolio work together to present a broad view of my computer science knowledge and growth. Although all three enhancements are based on the same thermostat project, each one highlights a different area of computer science. The software design and engineering enhancement shows my ability to refactor a monolithic project into a more modular and maintainable architecture. The algorithms and data structures enhancement demonstrates my ability to improve system behavior through better control logic using hysteresis. The database enhancement shows how I integrated persistent storage into the system and applied relational database concepts in a practical way.

Together, these artifacts reflect my ability to evaluate an existing project, identify weaknesses, and improve it from multiple technical perspectives. They also show that I can connect classroom learning to real-world software concerns such as maintainability, stability, monitoring, and structured data management. As a whole, this portfolio represents both the technical skills I developed throughout the Computer Science program and the professional growth that has prepared me for future opportunities in the field.
